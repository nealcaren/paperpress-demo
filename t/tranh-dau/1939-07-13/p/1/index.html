1789–1939…… One hundred and fifty years ago, the people of France rose up against the oppression of the nobility, overthrowing the monarchy and bringing down the feudal regime. The great Revolution of 1789 shook the entire world and began a glorious new era in the history of humanity’s liberation.

The French Revolution, like the great movements of society, was not a coincidence. Its foundations had been laid one or two hundred years earlier. Its cause lay in the socioeconomic structure, in the contradiction between the ruling class and the producing classes.

Let us recall the democratic freedoms won by the French people in the Revolution of 1789; the government of Daladier and the colonies have, in recent months, trampled those freedoms underfoot. Freedom of speech and thought is suppressed. Freedom of the press is subjected to raids. The freedom to strike is terrorized. Daladier prosecutes those who strike and imprisons the editors of the proletarian newspapers. In the colonies, the repression grows daily. The coolie workers who went on strike are thrown into prison for demanding higher wages and shorter working hours. The government is carrying out a policy of terror, arresting five administrators: Nguyễn-văn-Tiên-Nguyễn-văn-Quá (Tranh Đấu-La Lutte), Đào-hung-Long (Tháng Muòi), Ganofsky (Tia-Sáng), Huỳnh-văn-Thanh (Dân chüng), and comrade Phan-văn-Hùm, a member of the [illegible] council, for the crime of opposing national defense.
