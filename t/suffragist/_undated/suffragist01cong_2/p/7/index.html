The resolutions were enthusiastically carried by the whole meeting with but two dissenting votes.

The case for Woman Suffrage was eloquently stated at this meeting by Miss Jane Addams, Miss Margaret Hinchey, laundry worker; Miss Rose Winslow, weaver; Miss Mary Anderson, member of the National Sock and Shoe Maker's Union, and the distinguished woman Senator, Mrs. Helen Ring Robinson.

This Sunday meeting was the first of a series of packed evening meetings. On Monday evening, Mrs. Grace Wilbur Trout, Mrs. Medill McCormick, Mrs. Sherman Booth, Mrs. Ella S. Stewart, and Mrs. Catherine Waugh McCulloch of Chicago, told of the battle for Suffrage in Illinois, and the steps by which victory was achieved. Dr. Anna Howard Shaw devoted an impassioned and powerful address to the subject of Federal work for Woman Suffrage. Miss Lucy Burns, vice-chairman of the Congressional Committee, stated clearly and briefly the purpose of the meeting as follows

“The National Woman Suffrage Association is assembled in Washington to ask the Democratic party to enfranchise the women of America.

“Rarely in the history of the country has a party been more powerful than the Democratic party is today. It controls the executive offices, the Senate, and more than two-thirds of the members of the House of Representatives. It is in a position to give us effective and immediate help. “We ask the Democrats to take action now. Those who hold power are responsible to the country for

“We have in our hands today not only the weapon of a just cause; we have the support of ten enfranchised States. More than 3,600,000 women have a vote in Presidential elections. Itis unthinkable that a nationalGovernment which represents women and which appeals periodically to the suffrage of women, should ignore the issue of their right to political freedom.

“We can not wait until after the passage of scheduled administration reforms. These reforms, which affect women, should not be enacted without the consent of women. Congress is free to take action on our question in the present session. We ask the Administration to support the Woman Suffrage Amendment in Congress with its whole strength.”

Other propaganda meetings were held on Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday evenings. On Tuesday, the program was arranged by Mrs. Joseph Tilton Bowen, of Chicago. The subject of the evening was “The Position of Women Before the Law." The speakers on Wednesday evening were Mr. James Lees Laidlaw, president of the National Men's League for Woman Suffrage, Representative Victor Murdock, of Kansas; Representative Frank W. Mondell, of Wyoming; Mr. Gifford Pinchot; Representative William Kent, of California; Representative James W. Bryan, of Washington, Mr. Charles Edward Russell, and Judge John S. Crosby. On Thursday evening, Mrs. Carrie Chapman Catt spoke with Miss Harriet May Mills and Mrs. Thomas Hepburn.

The National Association can thus count on adequate financial provision for the work of the coming vyear.

The plan to make the literature department of the National Association a stock company, incorporated at $50,000, was launched with very little difficulty. Eleven thousand dollars to float the project was offered on the floor of the Convention within a few minutes.

The following officers were elected for the coming year: President, Dr. Anna Howard Shaw; First Vice- President, Miss Jane Addams, of Chicago; Second Vice-President, Mrs. Desha Breckenridge, of Kentucky; Third Vice-President, Miss Caroline Ruutz-Rees, of Connecticut; Recording Secretary, Mrs. Susan Walker Fitzgerald, of Massachusetts;, Corresponding Secretary, Mrs. Mary Ware Dennett, of New York; Treasurer, Mrs. Katherine Dexter McCormick, of Massachusetts; First Auditor, Mrs. Harriet Burton Laidlaw, of New York; Second Auditor, Mrs. Louise De- Koven Bowen, of Chicago.

A delightful reception, in honor of the officers of the National Association, to delegates and visitors attending the Convention, was given by Senator and Mrs. La Follette, in their home at 3320 Avenue of the Presidents.
